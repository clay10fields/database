# 37. Conclusions, and the hypotheses they support

Read `27-STANDING.md`, `36-TWO-LEADS.md`, and `FULL-TREATMENT.md` section 3 first. Nothing here changes `book/CURRENT-BOOK-2026-10-01.md`. Each new hypothesis has the one test that kills it.

## What the tests concluded

Forced flow is the wrong side. Shorting a funding spike, shorting a liquidation spike, fading a break, and shorting a perp-led rally lost at every hold that was run. The long of those same prints is the side that paid, and only when the print is wide or the crowd is already short.

A raw long is not an edge. A close above the prior 20-day high and a predicted-funding gap both looked positive until the coin-year average long was subtracted. Edge t fell to 0.70 and 0.52. Bull-year drift was the number.

The washout is the one daily long that survived the recheck. Spec in `22-WASHOUT-SPEC.md`: long-liquidations at the coin's own 90-day 95th, long/short ratio at its own 10th, at least four coins, hold 7 days, skip AAVE and SHIB. 141 trades, +7.00%, t 3.00. After the coin-year average, the shifted rerun's edge t was 2.36. It is a paper watch, not a book add. Kill it if the next 30 closed paper trades are red.

Breadth is the environmental variable that kept showing up. One coin at a 20-day high lost. Four or more coins at a high was bull drift. Four or more liquidation spikes, with the crowd already short, is the washout. Volume climax on a down day is 62% the same day as the liquidation spike. Do not add it as a second signal.

Regime is a size dial on the two book trades, not a new entry. Crowd short wants calm and downtrends. Flush long wants stress. Compressed volatility is the dead zone for both. Coins up over six months carry both. Coins in a multi-year decline fail both.

Spot flow sizes the book trades. It does not gate them. Gating cut return more than it cut drawdown.
