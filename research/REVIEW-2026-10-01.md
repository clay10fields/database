# Independent review of the 2026-10-01 branch work

Reviewer: Claude (cloud session), 2026-10-01. Brief: check that the status claims are honest, finish what was unfinished,
and re-check the corrections. Everything below was re-run from the panel in this session, not read off the tables.

## Verdict
**The work is real, the numbers reproduce, and the write-ups are mostly honest.** Fifteen `chatgpt-*` branches hold
Steps 17 and 19-28 plus the LIQF re-admission; none are on `main`. The status summary was accurate about what is done and
accurate about what is blocked, with one exception (token unlocks, below).

Three findings. One matters.

## 1. The headline risk number is a property of seven hand-picked coins — and that caveat was dropped
**Severity: this is the finding.**

`CURRENT-BOOK-2026-10-01.md` calls itself the authoritative build spec and reports CAGR 93.87% at **-12.94%** max
drawdown. It gives the Flush-B universe as seven names (XLM SOL XRP HBAR AVAX AAVE BCH) with no derivation.

Re-running `universe-refresh/code/dynamic_universe_test.py`:

| universe | CAGR | max DD | Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| dynamic CS + curated Flush | 93.94% | **-12.94%** | 2.659 |
| dynamic CS + dynamic Flush | 94.15% | **-26.83%** | 2.451 |

The return is the same either way. **The drawdown doubles.** So -12.94% is not a measurement of the strategy, it is a
measurement of the seven names — and those names were chosen after seeing per-coin results. Step 17 independently points
the same way: off-universe, on faded and delisted controls, Flush-B's pooled edge is +0.76% against +2.93% on the curated
seven.

Step 27 said this clearly — "an unresolved model-selection constraint, not a causal universe solution", "a validated
research set, not a general universe law". **The caveat existed and then did not survive the trip into the authoritative
file.** Anyone reading only `CURRENT-BOOK` or `AUDIT-STATUS` — which is what they are for — would have taken -12.94% as
the book's risk profile. Both files now carry it.

Not a reason to drop Flush-B. It is the reason Monte Carlo's "plan for -30%" is the number to size against, and the
reason Flush-B's live record has to carry more weight than CS72's. Combined with Step 19 (Flush-B just misses the
Bonferroni threshold that CS72 clears), the two engines are not equally established and the book should stop presenting
them as if they were.

## 2. Token unlocks: declared blocked, actually run, and it supports his hypothesis
`AUDIT-STATUS` said the subtest was "blocked by lack of a verified point-in-time historical unlock calendar" with nothing
run. `research/token-unlocks/` holds three scripts, an event list, and eleven result tables — and no knowledge file, so
the result was invisible.

What it found, on 45 events (2023-2025): excess of BTC is **-5.33% in the final week** before an unlock against -1.64%
the week before, acceleration -3.69 pp at t -2.72, and the three days *after* the unlock are flat (-0.07%). Both halves of
the sample agree. That is exactly the shape he predicted — the selling is in front of the unlock, not after it.

It is a **LEAD**: n 45 against the 200 bar, t 2.72 against 3.0, dose-response weak (Spearman -0.13). And the real block is
narrower than stated: the event list is a curated third-party dataset whose event-level selection is undocumented, and a
trade that enters 7-14 days early cannot run off a list assembled after the fact. The calendar is what is missing, not
the result. Written up in `token-unlocks/TOKEN-UNLOCKS.md`.

One useful detail: a hard 10% stop caps the tail at -10% (unstopped worst trade: **-70.5%**) without costing anything on
the mean — the opposite of every mean-reversion long in this repo, and consistent with this being a short into supply.

* **Step 19 is honest about Flush-B missing the threshold.** It would have been easy to bury.
* **Step 17 labels itself inconclusive** rather than claiming survivorship is solved.
* **Step 20 declined a FOMC pause** on a direct portfolio counterfactual rather than on the flattering 17-trade subset.
* **Step 28 declined a regime-transition throttle** and flagged the 2022-23 / 2024-26 sign flip as forward-monitoring
  only instead of converting a post-hoc observation into a rule.

That pattern — repeatedly declining the attractive post-hoc rule — is why I believe the rest of these numbers.
